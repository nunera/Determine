AA cane finds the ground.
The branch is at head height.

About 1 in 8 blind travellers hit their head at least once a month.

In a survey of 307 blind travellers, 13% had a head-level accident at least monthly. 86% of head-level accidents happened outdoors, mostly on tree branches, and 23% had medical consequences.2 A long cane sweeps the ground ahead of you. Research classes it as a primary aid,1 and it is excellent at curbs and steps. It cannot feel a branch at eye level, an open cabinet door or a truck mirror.

The long cane “does not protect users from obstacles at the upper body and head levels.”
Pittet et al., Scientific Reports, 20261

Keeps her cane

Determine adds a head-level layer. It never replaces the cane or a guide dog, and it never says the path is clear.

Wants a warning, not a narrator

In a 2026 trial, every participant preferred short, precise output (“person to your left at 11 o'clock, 3 metres”) to generative descriptions.1

Learns it in minutes

4 of 13 trial users struggled with a rival's spatialised sounds.1 Determine uses a small cue set, taught at setup, with direction carried twice.

Determine · the problem2 / 8

BDumb glasses. Your phone does the thinking.

Sense · small front, boards behind

The ESP32-CAM sends JPEG frames; one compact VL53L5CX Mini sends 64 distances through the rear-mounted C3. Two independent Wi-Fi streams reach the phone, which makes every hazard decision.

Decide · on the phone

The phone aligns the two streams by timestamp and receive age, then an on-device object detector plus the distance grid feed fixed hazard rules and pick one cue: which sound, how loud on each side, which temple buzzes.

Play · on the glasses

The C3 plays a stored sound straight to the bone over I2S, so urgent beeps skip Bluetooth audio delay. Every command expires: a late one is dropped, never played late.

Range plus context. The 8×8 ToF grid measures distance even when a branch or open door has no detector label; the camera adds object context.

Same app, other glasses. Meta's Wearables DAT 1.0 can stream supported glasses' camera to a phone; its Mock Device Kit simulates it.9 Determine's frame input could accept Meta frames. Claim only if built at HackTX.

Two boards, one hotspot. The C3 has no camera or Espressif parallel-camera driver support,10 so a separate ESP32-CAM supplies JPEG once its sensor and PSRAM are checked. Both join the phone hotspot; the phone drops stale frames and grids after clock-offset and receive-age checks. No inter-board wire.

Weight off the nose. Only camera and 12.7 × 25.4 mm Mini ToF face forward; C3, amps and drivers go on a rear strap, power bank in a pocket.7 Fit and comfort remain untested. Two separately powered boards share one phone hotspot.

How close → tempo

The car-sensor model everyone already knows. Never above 6 notes a second.3

Where → which ear

Left staff plays the left ear, right staff the right. The matching temple buzzes too.

What → timbre

Timbre and rhythm are the strongest cues for telling sounds apart.3

Words → optional

Sped-up spoken words (spearcons) after the beep, never instead of it.4

There is no all-clear sound. Silence is a rest, not the end of the piece.

Silence would mean “nothing to report from a working system”, never “path clear”. If the camera, distance grid or either board's link fails, the design targets a descending system off motif within one second, repeated until recovery. If the cue board loses power, the glasses cannot beep: the phone must speak or vibrate the failure instead. Timing and audibility are untested. Tempo, motif and haptics carry urgency too.

Determine · the cue language4 / 8

DEqually loud in both ears, even when one is weaker.

Uses the audiology convention (X left, O right) but it is not a hearing test and never shows results as one. Values are illustrative.

Two to three minutes at setup, a 20-second check each time you put them on.

  1. FitSeat the frame. The app notes which output is in use and which side feels weaker.
  2. Find each side's quietest audible levelAn adaptive staircase, tap “heard” or “not heard”, in the three bands the cues use.
  3. Set the level10–20 dB above that threshold, your choice, clamped to the ceiling.3
  4. BalanceThe real obstacle cue alternates left and right. Nudge until both feel the same.
  5. VerifyName the side of five random cues. Four right keeps stereo; otherwise switch mode.

Stereo

Each ear gets its own calibrated level. Side is heard, and the matching temple confirms it.

One deaf ear

Bone-conducted sound crosses the skull losing only about 0–10 dB,5 so left/right audio can't carry direction. Audio goes the same to both sides; the temple buzz and words carry direction.

Haptic only

For anyone who prefers it. No calibration step, slider or urgency boost ever passes the ceiling; if the weak side would need more, it's marked unusable.

Determine · calibration5 / 8

ETwo loops. One can't wait. One can think.

Warnings never touch the network. Scene description is a question Maya chooses to ask; the design target is AWS's Annapurna silicon: a Graviton API and a small vision-language model on Inferentia2. Trainium2 is the documented alternative if the Inf2 path fails.11,13–15

Reflex phone · offline · always on

Target ≤ 250 ms from capture to cue at the 95th percentile, shown live on the judges' screen. It's a goal until we measure it, and nothing is measured yet — the shared hotspot carrying two streams is the least certain part. At walking pace (1.4 m/s), 250 ms is about 35 cm, so warning distances budget for it.

Three tiers of silicon, placed by latency, power and failure budget: two ESP32-class boards on the glasses (a C3 for grid, cues and haptics6; a classic-ESP32 camera10), the phone for every decision, and Annapurna silicon for the one job that can wait seconds.

Describe on request · Annapurna silicon

  1. AskLong-press: what's ahead? Never automatic.
  2. RedactThe phone would blur faces and sensitive details before upload; imperfect redaction is a privacy risk, so upload requires the user's choice.
  3. GravitonA small arm64 Lambda API (Graviton2) would hold the prompt: two sentences, surroundings first, no guesses about people.17
  4. Inferentia2SmolVLM on one inf2.xlarge ($0.76/h) using Hugging Face Optimum Neuron. The model family is listed for Inf2; this deployment is still untested by us.14,15
  5. SpeakThe phone would say may be wrong, then read the result. Any mid- or near-range hazard interrupts it.

GWhat it can't see, said out loud.

  • Glass doors and windows light passes through
  • Thin wires and ropes too small for 8×8
  • Drop-offs, curbs, stairs going down the cane's job
  • Anything outside the forward view 63° diagonal
  • Fast-moving vehicles not a traffic aid
  • Glare, darkness, rain and fog degrades, announced
  • Anything beyond about 3 m by design

Spoken to every new user during setup. Demos run on a staged indoor course with a sighted spotter.

  • Fail loud.Design target: announce “system off” within one second through a working output, including the phone if the frame loses power. Never call silence a clear path. Not yet tested.
  • Unknown is not clear.No detections never produces a reassuring sound.
