Determine

Dumb glasses, a capable phone, and a calibrated warning about what is directly ahead of you.

Architecture and prototype contract
Status: proposed design. Nothing here is built, clinically validated, or cleared as a medical device. It does not replace a white cane, a guide dog or orientation-and-mobility training.

WearableTwo dumb boards: an ESP32-CAM that streams JPEG, and a XIAO ESP32-C3 carrying the time-of-flight array, stereo bone-conduction cues and two haptic motors. No AI on the frame.
PhoneDoes all perception, decides which hazard to report, runs calibration and the user interface. Android first.
UsersBlind and low-vision travellers, including people with unilateral or asymmetric hearing loss.
TargetHackTX 26, Oct 24–25 2026, UT Austin. Build on site within the event rules.
Pitch. When you reverse a car, your view is limited, so the car gives you parking sensors. People who are actually blind or have low vision mostly do without a head-level equivalent. Determine is that sensor for a walker: it beeps faster as an obstacle gets closer, places the warning on the left, centre or right, and calibrates its loudness so both sides are heard equally, even with hearing loss in one ear.

Chapter 1

Mission, guarantees and non-goals

1.1 The precise product

Determine is a secondary electronic travel aid (ETA). It adds a near-field warning layer, focused on chest- and head-level obstacles, on top of the traveller's primary aid. The 2026 Scientific Reports study defines primary aids as the white cane and guide dog, and ETAs as supplementary tools [2]. A long cane covers the ground and "does not protect users from obstacles at the upper body and head levels" [2]. In a survey of 307 blind travellers, 13% had a head-level accident at least monthly, 86% of head-level accidents happened outdoors (mostly tree branches), and 23% of head-level accidents had medical consequences [3]. FACT

The wearable is deliberately "dumb": it has sensors, radios and actuators, but it makes no decisions. The phone app is the processing unit. It decides what counts as a hazard, which single cue to play and how loud each side should be. DESIGN

1.2 Guarantees

  1. G1 Fail loud. If the camera, ToF, link, phone app or wearable power fails, the system targets an audible or tactile "system unavailable" notification through a surviving output within ≤ 1 s PROPOSED. If the cue board loses power, the phone uses its own speaker and vibration: the camera board has no audio or haptic output, so a dead cue board leaves the frame unable to play an earcon. Silence means "nothing to report from a working system", never "path clear".
  2. G2 Unknown is not clear. An empty detection list never produces a reassurance cue. Determine has no "all clear" sound.
  3. G3 Offline hazard loop. Capture → perception → decision → cue runs with no internet connection.
  4. G4 Calibrated, capped output. Per-side gain comes from the wearer's own calibration and is clamped by a ceiling that no setting can exceed.
  5. G5 Redundant direction. Every directional cue is carried by at least two channels the wearer has confirmed during calibration that they can perceive.
  6. G6 Bounded vocabulary. Hazard cues come from a small, learnable set (Chapter 8). Free-form speech never pre-empts a hazard cue.
  7. G7 Privacy by default. Frames stay in phone memory. Images are not retained or uploaded unless the user explicitly turns on a separate feature.

Chapter 2

Evidence base: travellers, hearing and cues

2.1 What a real ETA trial shows

Pittet et al. (2026) tested 13 blind and low-vision participants on an indoor corridor with floor, waist and head-level obstacles. They compared cane alone with cane plus NOA (a camera-based harness) and cane plus BuzzClip (ultrasonic) [2]. FACT

Measure (mean)Cane onlyCane + NOACane + BuzzClip
Body contacts with obstacles2.921.623.54
Cane contacts4.692.623.15

What this means for Determine:

  • Precision beats volume. Participants rated NOA higher on precision and on upper-body detection. The BuzzClip's vibrations were criticised as too weak and too frequent, and gave no improvement in incidents [2].
  • Learnability matters. 4 of 13 participants had trouble understanding NOA's spatialised warning sounds [2]. Determine therefore uses a small set of cues, trained during onboarding, with direction carried redundantly.
  • Short, precise beats generative. For finding objects, every participant preferred concise output such as "Person to your left at 11 o'clock, 3 metres" from a local deep-learning model over generative-AI descriptions [2].
  • Test with the real population. Of 140 ETA studies reviewed, only 29% included blind or low-vision participants [2]. A blindfolded sighted tester does not validate Determine.

2.2 The glasses premise, without stereotypes

Some blind people wear dark glasses, but many do not. Blindness is a spectrum, and many legally blind people use their remaining vision. Determine therefore offers clear, tinted and clip-on configurations, and its sensors mount on the frame front so that lens tint does not affect sensing. DESIGN A pair of glasses is a natural place for the hardware: it sits at eye level, it points where the head points, and it already rests on the temples, where bone conduction and haptics make contact.

2.3 Hearing: two different problems

Asymmetric hearing loss

Both ears hear, but one hears less. Per-side gain can equalise perceived loudness, which is exactly what the user asked for. Calibration is the right tool here. INFERENCE

Single-sided deafness (SSD)

One cochlea does no useful work. Every left or right channel ends up at the same working cochlea. In 28 unilaterally deaf subjects, sound crossing the skull lost only about 0–10 dB, depending on frequency, with roughly 40 dB variation between people [26]. Left/right audio therefore cannot carry direction, so haptics and spoken words must. FACT → DESIGN

  • Bone-conduction headsets lateralise sound much like headphones in a small study. Even a 20 dB level difference gave only about 75% correct lateralisation [27]. Stereo bone conduction is useful, but it is not reliable enough to be the only carrier of direction. FACT
  • In one study, a contralateral-routing bone-conduction device did not improve localisation for people with single-sided deafness [28]. Monaural listeners rely heavily on head-shadow level cues [29]. FACT
  • Audio played through bone-conduction headphones increased localisation errors for real environmental sounds [52]. Determine keeps cues short and sparse so it does not mask traffic and voices. FACT → DESIGN

2.4 Auditory-display findings used in Chapter 8

  • Earcon guidelines (Brewster et al.): vary timbre, register and rhythm, and use musical timbres rather than sine waves. Make rhythms differ substantially, including in note count. Keep pitch roughly within 125–150 Hz to 5 kHz. Notes should last at least 0.0825 s, at most about 6 notes per second, with about 0.1 s between serial earcons. Keep intensity 10–20 dB above threshold. Do not rely on register or intensity alone for absolute identification [24]. FACT
  • Spearcons, which are sped-up spoken words, beat earcons and auditory icons on menu-navigation efficiency and accuracy, and are learned about as fast as speech [25]. FACT
  • Safe listening: WHO lists 80 dB as safe for up to 40 hours a week and 90 dB for only 4 hours. It recommends keeping devices at or below 60% of maximum volume [4]. The WHO-ITU standard defines exposure modes for adults and sensitive users [5]. FACT

3.1 Where Determine sits

None of the products found combines all four of the following: a low-cost glasses form, phone-side perception, bone-conduction and haptic output, and explicit calibration for asymmetric hearing. No ETA product or paper found addresses unilateral hearing loss in its cue design. That is an inference from absence of evidence, not a verified gap. INFERENCE

Not "AI glasses for the blind"

That framing is crowded and invites overclaiming. Determine is a parking sensor for walking, whose output follows a defined set of rules.

Commodity hardware, unpriced build

The phone does perception; the frame uses two commodity boards. Only the Mini ToF has a price rechecked for this list ($25.95 [20]). Micro Center prices for the boards, amps, haptic parts and regulator are unverified, and power and mounts remain unpriced (Chapter 12). Do not quote a frame total yet.

Hearing-aware by construction

Calibration, a mono-safe mode and haptic direction are core features, not settings bolted on later.
Claims to avoid in the pitch: "replaces the cane", "detects stairs, curbs or traffic", "makes navigation safe", "restores hearing", "first obstacle sensor for the blind" (WeWALK and NOA already exist), "uses Auracast" (unless it was actually built and demonstrated).

There is no board-to-board bus: the phone is the only integrator. The phone estimates a separate clock offset for each board, timestamps receipt, and discards FRAME or RANGE whose offset-corrected capture time or receive age exceeds the freshness bound. The cue board enforces the gain ceiling independently. It compares t_issued_phone_us + ttl_ms against its offset-corrected current time, dropping a delayed CUE rather than resetting its lifetime on arrival. The C3's watchdog plays its system-off cue when phone PINGs stop while the board is powered; the phone uses its own speaker/vibration if a board disappears. Clock uncertainty, command expiry and both failure directions must be tested in Gates G6–G7. Power-bank battery percentage is not reported by either board unless external sensing is added; loss of its power is detected by missing heartbeats on the phone. DESIGN

5.3 Output hardware

  • Stereo bone conduction, played on the glasses. Two MAX98357A I²S amplifiers share one I²S bus. Select left on one amp and right on the other: the default mixes both channels [36]. Each drives one 8 Ω / 1 W Adafruit 1674 transducer. Adafruit's own store was out of stock on 18 Oct, but DigiKey lists the same item (1528-1948-ND); its displayed inventory is not a checkout-confirmed delivery date. Limit output below the transducer's 1 W continuous rating, and cover each skin-facing transducer and wire joint against sweat [36]. FACT → DESIGN
  • Haptics. Two ERM disc motors, one per side, each driven by a DRV2605L. Both drivers have the same I²C address [36], so the selected TCA9548A mux isolates them on separate channels; the Mini ToF may use the upstream bus [59]. The Micro Center driver listing may differ in connector style from Adafruit's current STEMMA QT revision: inspect it and wire its header as supplied. If a DRV2605L fails at bring-up, the optional fallback in §12.2 drives each motor through a logic-level MOSFET with a flyback diode from a spare C3 PWM GPIO: simple pulses only, no driver waveforms, with the phone-side ceiling and cadence rules unchanged. DESIGN
  • Fallback audio. A commercial bone-conduction headset paired to the phone, such as Shokz OpenMove at a $79.95 list price (variants showed sold out) or OpenRun at $129.95 [37]. Classic Bluetooth adds latency (§6.2), so in this mode haptics carry the urgent cue.

5.4 Power

The revision uses two Wi-Fi boards and a single compact front-facing Mini ToF board, not three 41 × 25 mm Modulino Distance boards. The Mini measures 12.7 × 25.4 mm, retains the 8×8 grid and 4 m vendor range [20], and is the justified shipped exception to Houston-first sourcing. The ESP32-CAM and ToF face forward; the C3, amplifiers, haptic drivers and power distribution sit toward the back of a headband. The power bank stays in a pocket, feeding the C3 and a separate 5 V distribution branch for the camera, amps and a 3.3 V LM3671 peripheral regulator. All grounds needed for shared signals are common. The board-fit, thermal load and simultaneous output current are unmeasured; Gate G0 checks power and mounting before any wearable claim. DESIGN

GPIO budget: the C3 has one hardware I²C bus [59] for the Mini ToF and TCA9548A; the two DRV2605Ls sit on separate mux channels. The two amplifiers share three I²S signals. Fix the complete pin map, status-LED GPIO, two spare PWM GPIOs for the MOSFET haptic fallback and channel-select resistor values before soldering; do not power motors or amps from an unmeasured C3 3V3 output. Chapter 7

Perception and hazard policy

7.1 Pipeline

  1. Ingest and freshness gate. Two independent producers: the ESP32-CAM's frames and the cue board's ToF grids each carry their board's clock, a per-board offset estimated by PING/PONG, and the phone's receive timestamp. Drop any input whose offset-corrected capture time or receive age exceeds the freshness bound. Every stage records a timestamp, for Gate G6.
  2. Range corridor (class-agnostic). Split the 8×8 ToF grid into a corridor (central columns, head-to-chest rows) and left and right flanks. Use the minimum valid distance per region. Invalid zones count as UNKNOWN.
  3. Semantics. MediaPipe Object Detector in LIVE_STREAM mode with a category allowlist and a score threshold [21], running on the LiteRT runtime [22]. Classes are used for labels ("person", "door", "chair") and for bearing, never as the only trigger for a near-field hazard.
  4. Fusion. Match detections to ToF regions by bearing. If a ToF return lies in the corridor and no detection matches, report it as a generic "obstacle".
  5. Proximity band. FAR / MID / NEAR come from ToF distance when it is valid. Without ToF, use only relative cues such as bounding-box growth over time, and never speak metres.
  6. Policy. Pick at most one alert, with persistence and hysteresis (below).

7.2 Hazard policy (v0 rules)

inputs: corridor_min_mm, left_min_mm, right_min_mm (valid flags), detections, head_yaw_rate
band(d): NEAR if d < 1000; MID if d < 2000; FAR if d < 3000; else NONE      # PROPOSED mm
enter alert:  same region ∧ band ≥ FAR for ≥ 3 consecutive ToF samples (~200 ms @15 Hz)
exit alert:   band NONE for ≥ 5 samples, or region changes (re-enter with new cue)
priority:     NEAR corridor > NEAR flank > MID corridor > MID flank > FAR corridor
rate limit:   ≤ 1 alert onset / 700 ms; repeat cadence set by band (Chapter 8)
suppress:     head_yaw_rate high (scanning) → hold FAR cues; NEAR always passes
label:        matched detection class if confident, else "obstacle"
never:        output CLEAR; speak distance without valid ToF; let description pre-empt NEAR

The bands are PROPOSED. With 250 ms of system latency and about 1 s of human reaction at 1.4 m/s, a hazard reported at 1.0 m leaves very little margin. That is why the FAR and MID bands exist, and why the NEAR band is a last-moment warning, not the main mechanism. INFERENCE White-cane mode MAY suppress low-zone returns that the cane already covers. The Ara wearable uses a similar mode [1].

7.3 Optional scene description (Describe loop)

The user asks with a long press or voice: "What's ahead?" — never automatically. If the user has opted into cloud description and the service is available, the phone redacts faces and sensitive detail before upload; imperfect redaction is a privacy risk, so upload happens only on the user's explicit choice. A small arm64 Lambda API on Graviton2 [65] holds the prompt — two sentences, surroundings first, no guesses about people — and a small vision-language model describes the image: SmolVLM via Hugging Face Optimum Neuron on one Inf2 instance [62][63]. Optimum Neuron lists SmolVLM, and Gemma3 as a second candidate, for image-text-to-text on Inf2 with vLLM support [62], but neither deployment is tested by us. If no description service is ready, the phone says description is unavailable; it does not improvise an untested local model. A returned answer is prefixed "may be wrong". MID or NEAR hazards interrupt it, and it is inactive while the system is UNAVAILABLE. Response time is unmeasured; the design plans for seconds, not milliseconds. DESIGN

Chapter 9

Calibration for asymmetric hearing

Goal. Both sides of the cue should be equally perceptible to this wearer, with this frame position and this output device, without ever exceeding a hard ceiling. This is perceptual matching. It is not a hearing test or a diagnosis, and it does not restore hearing. DESIGN

  1. Fit and device. The app asks the wearer to seat the frame. It records which output device is in use (glasses transducers or a headset) and the wearer's self-reported weaker side. It warns about ambient noise if the phone microphone reads high.
  2. Per-side detection threshold. An adaptive 2-down-1-up staircase, which converges near 70.7% correct [57], starts from a quiet level. The wearer answers "heard / not heard" by tapping. It runs in three bands that match the cue timbres (for example 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz) and stops after 6–8 reversals. PROPOSED
  3. Presentation level. Per band and side, the level is threshold plus a sensation level chosen by the wearer between 10 and 20 dB [24], clamped to the ceiling.
  4. Loudness balance. Alternate the real "obstacle" cue left and right. The wearer nudges a balance control until both sides feel equally loud, which corrects for cue spectrum and for the bone crossing the skull. Repeat once to check consistency.
  5. Verify and choose a mode. Play five random-side cues and have the wearer name the side.
    • Stereo mode: at least 4 of 5 correct and no side at the ceiling.
    • SSD mode: one side cannot be detected at the ceiling, or side naming is at chance. Audio becomes identical on both sides (mono). Direction moves to haptics plus spearcons.
    • Haptic-only mode: for wearers who prefer it or cannot use the audio.

9.1 Profile and safety rules

HearingProfile {
  device_id, created_at, fit_note,
  mode: STEREO | SSD | HAPTIC_ONLY,

  bands: [{hz, thr_db_L, thr_db_R, sl_db, offset_db_L, offset_db_R}],

  verified: {trials:5, correct:n}
}
  • Ceiling first. No calibration step, slider or urgency step can exceed ceiling_db. If the weak side would need more, it is marked unusable. The app does not push more energy into it. DESIGN
  • Re-run every session, because how the frame couples to the head changes each time it is worn. A 20-second quick check suffices if the previous profile is under 7 days old. PROPOSED
  • Dose. Track cumulative cue-on time. Cues are short and sparse by design. Until the transducers are measured on an artificial mastoid, Determine makes no claim of equivalence to the WHO and ITU air-conduction limits [4][5].
  • Professional care. The app recommends an audiologist for anyone with known hearing loss and never displays results as audiometry.
  • OS features stay reachable. Android mono audio and balance settings remain available. Determine's profile works alongside them, not as a replacement.

11.4 Three-minute judge demo

  1. Hook (20 s): the parking-sensor line, moved from the bumper to the brow. Hold up the two-board frame.
  2. Live course (75 s): a sighted spotter walks the wearer past a head-height foam bar, a person, and a left-side then right-side obstacle. The judge diagnostics screen shows both streams' age, the 8×8 ToF grid, the chosen alert and measured latency.
  3. One-ear mode (30 s): switch the profile to SSD mode live. Audio goes mono; the urgent cue stays audible and direction moves to haptics plus spearcons. Show the ceiling stopping a forced gain increase.
  4. Pull the plug (20 s): unplug the camera board. The system-off cue plays within the measured bound; reconnect and wait for READY. No "clear" is ever heard.
  5. Ask (20 s): after recovery, long-press "What's ahead?" If the Describe loop is ready, a redacted frame returns a two-sentence answer prefixed "may be wrong". If accelerator access failed, skip the answer and keep showing the offline warning loop.
  6. Close (15 s): the logged p50/p95 latency, the miss and false-alert table, what it cannot see, and what comes next.
