Determine

Dumb glasses, a capable phone, and a calibrated warning about what is directly ahead of you.

Architecture and prototype contract
Status: proposed design. Nothing here is built, clinically validated, or cleared as a medical device. It does not replace a white cane, a guide dog or orientation-and-mobility training.

WearableTwo dumb boards: an ESP32-CAM that streams JPEG, and a XIAO ESP32-C3 carrying one forward time-of-flight zone and the two temple haptic drivers. No AI on the frame, and no audio hardware on it either: cue sounds are synthesised on the phone and played through a finished bone-conduction Bluetooth headset.
PhoneDoes all perception, decides which hazard to report, runs calibration and the user interface. Android first.
UsersBlind and low-vision travellers, including people with unilateral or asymmetric hearing loss.
TargetHackTX 26, Oct 24–25 2026, UT Austin. Build on site within the event rules.
Pitch. When you reverse a car, your view is limited, so the car gives you parking sensors. People who are actually blind or have low vision mostly do without a head-level equivalent. Determine is that sensor for a walker: it beeps faster as an obstacle gets closer, places the warning on the left, centre or right, and calibrates its loudness so both sides are heard equally, even with hearing loss in one ear.

Chapter 1

Mission, guarantees and non-goals

1.1 The precise product

Determine is a secondary electronic travel aid (ETA). It adds a near-field warning layer, focused on chest- and head-level obstacles, on top of the traveller's primary aid. The 2026 Scientific Reports study defines primary aids as the white cane and guide dog, and ETAs as supplementary tools [2]. A long cane covers the ground and "does not protect users from obstacles at the upper body and head levels" [2]. In a survey of 307 blind travellers, 13% had a head-level accident at least monthly, 86% of head-level accidents happened outdoors (mostly tree branches), and 23% of head-level accidents had medical consequences [3]. FACT

The wearable is deliberately "dumb": it has sensors, radios and actuators, but it makes no decisions. The phone app is the processing unit. It decides what counts as a hazard, which single cue to play and how loud each side should be. DESIGN

1.2 Guarantees

  1. G1 Fail loud. If the camera, ToF, link, headset route, phone app or wearable power fails, the system targets an audible or tactile "system unavailable" notification through a surviving output within ≤ 1 s PROPOSED. The phone owns audio, so losing the headset route is itself a fault: it is announced explicitly (and the temples keep buzzing if the cue board is alive); the app never silently reroutes cue audio to the phone speaker. Silence means "nothing to report from a working system", never "path clear".
  2. G2 Unknown is not clear. An empty detection list never produces a reassurance cue. Determine has no "all clear" sound.
  3. G3 Offline hazard loop. Capture → perception → decision → cue runs with no internet connection.
  4. G4 Calibrated, capped output. Per-side listening level comes from the wearer's own calibration and is clamped by a phone-enforced ceiling that no setting can exceed; the board separately clamps haptic intensity to haptic_max.
  5. G5 Redundant urgency. Every warning travels on at least two channels the wearer has confirmed during calibration that they can perceive (headset audio plus temple haptics). Direction is not carried at all in v0, rather than carried badly.
  6. G6 Bounded vocabulary. Hazard cues come from a small, learnable set (Chapter 8). Free-form speech never pre-empts a hazard cue.
  7. G7 Privacy by default. Frames stay in phone memory. Images are not retained or uploaded unless the user explicitly turns on a separate feature.

Chapter 2

Evidence base: travellers, hearing and cues

2.1 What a real ETA trial shows

Pittet et al. (2026) tested 13 blind and low-vision participants on an indoor corridor with floor, waist and head-level obstacles. They compared cane alone with cane plus NOA (a camera-based harness) and cane plus BuzzClip (ultrasonic) [2]. FACT

Measure (mean)Cane onlyCane + NOACane + BuzzClip
Body contacts with obstacles2.921.623.54
Cane contacts4.692.623.15

What this means for Determine:

  • Precision beats volume. Participants rated NOA higher on precision and on upper-body detection. The BuzzClip's vibrations were criticised as too weak and too frequent, and gave no improvement in incidents [2].
  • Learnability matters. 4 of 13 participants had trouble understanding NOA's spatialised warning sounds [2]. Determine therefore uses a small set of cues, trained during onboarding, with direction carried redundantly.
  • Short, precise beats generative. For finding objects, every participant preferred concise output such as "Person to your left at 11 o'clock, 3 metres" from a local deep-learning model over generative-AI descriptions [2].
  • Test with the real population. Of 140 ETA studies reviewed, only 29% included blind or low-vision participants [2]. A blindfolded sighted tester does not validate Determine.

2.2 The glasses premise, without stereotypes

Some blind people wear dark glasses, but many do not. Blindness is a spectrum, and many legally blind people use their remaining vision. Determine therefore offers clear, tinted and clip-on configurations, and its sensors mount on the frame front so that lens tint does not affect sensing. DESIGN A pair of glasses is a natural place for the hardware: it sits at eye level, it points where the head points, and it already rests on the temples, where bone conduction and haptics make contact.

2.3 Hearing: two different problems

Asymmetric hearing loss

Both ears hear, but one hears less. Per-side gain can equalise perceived loudness, which is exactly what the user asked for. Calibration is the right tool here. INFERENCE

Single-sided deafness (SSD)

One cochlea does no useful work. Every left or right channel ends up at the same working cochlea. In 28 unilaterally deaf subjects, sound crossing the skull lost only about 0–10 dB, depending on frequency, with roughly 40 dB variation between people [26]. Left/right audio therefore cannot carry direction, so haptics and spoken words must. FACT → DESIGN

  • Bone-conduction headsets lateralise sound much like headphones in a small study. Even a 20 dB level difference gave only about 75% correct lateralisation [27]. Stereo bone conduction is useful, but it is not reliable enough to be the only carrier of direction. FACT
  • In one study, a contralateral-routing bone-conduction device did not improve localisation for people with single-sided deafness [28]. Monaural listeners rely heavily on head-shadow level cues [29]. FACT
  • Audio played through bone-conduction headphones increased localisation errors for real environmental sounds [52]. Determine keeps cues short and sparse so it does not mask traffic and voices. FACT → DESIGN

2.4 Auditory-display findings used in Chapter 8

  • Earcon guidelines (Brewster et al.): vary timbre, register and rhythm, and use musical timbres rather than sine waves. Make rhythms differ substantially, including in note count. Keep pitch roughly within 125–150 Hz to 5 kHz. Notes should last at least 0.0825 s, at most about 6 notes per second, with about 0.1 s between serial earcons. Keep intensity 10–20 dB above threshold. Do not rely on register or intensity alone for absolute identification [24]. FACT
  • Spearcons, which are sped-up spoken words, beat earcons and auditory icons on menu-navigation efficiency and accuracy, and are learned about as fast as speech [25]. FACT
  • Safe listening: WHO lists 80 dB as safe for up to 40 hours a week and 90 dB for only 4 hours. It recommends keeping devices at or below 60% of maximum volume [4]. The WHO-ITU standard defines exposure modes for adults and sensitive users [5]. FACT

3.1 Where Determine sits

None of the products found combines all four of the following: a low-cost glasses form, phone-side perception, bone-conduction and haptic output, and explicit calibration for asymmetric hearing. No ETA product or paper found addresses unilateral hearing loss in its cue design. That is an inference from absence of evidence, not a verified gap. INFERENCE

Not "AI glasses for the blind"

That framing is crowded and invites overclaiming. Determine is a parking sensor for walking, whose output follows a defined set of rules.

Commodity hardware, one store

Two boards, one forward ToF zone and two intended temple haptics. Houston stock was checked in Zen; the motor shortage, sold-out regulator and unresolved integrated audio prevent a complete-build purchase claim (Chapter 12).

Hearing-aware by construction

Per-side calibration, a mono-safe mode for single-sided deafness and haptic confirmation are core features, not settings bolted on later.

There is no board-to-board bus: the phone is the only integrator. The phone estimates a separate clock offset for each board, timestamps receipt, and discards FRAME or RANGE whose offset-corrected capture time or receive age exceeds the freshness bound. The cue board compares deadline_board_us against its offset-corrected current time, dropping a late CUE (ACK EXPIRED) rather than resetting its lifetime on arrival; deadline_board_us = 0 means no sync (ACK NO_SYNC). One CUE drives both legs of one cue: the phone synthesises the sound for the headset while the board plays the haptic pattern and acknowledges. The C3's watchdog runs haptics only — LSS on both temples, capped at haptic_max — when phone PINGs stop while the board is powered; the phone announces a dead board explicitly on its own outputs. Clock uncertainty, command expiry and both failure directions must be tested in Gates G6–G7. Power-bank battery percentage is not reported by either board unless external sensing is added; loss of its power is detected by missing heartbeats on the phone. DESIGN

5.3 Output hardware

  • Cue audio: implemented headset path, integrated hardware unresolved. The phone synthesises cues and supports Bluetooth headset playback with per-side ceilings and explicit route-failure handling; it never silently falls back to its speaker. The separate Shokz OpenRun is not approved for the intended build and its old listing redirects to support [41]. No suitable stocked discrete bone-conduction transducer was identified; the Humbird listing says “No longer carried”. No raw-transducer/amplifier replacement is selected. Classic Bluetooth latency [16] remains a bench-path consideration, not proof of final hardware. OPEN DESIGN GATE
  • Haptics. Two temple motors, one per side, each driven by a DRV2605L (§12.2: DRV2605L ×2, ERM motor ×2, TCA9548A mux ×1). Both drivers share I²C address 0x5A [36], so the TCA9548A mux isolates them on separate channels; the VL53L4CD keeps its own default address on the upstream bus [20]. In v0 both temples fire together for every forward hazard — the single zone has no lateral resolution — while per-side intensity remains available for calibration verification and future lateral sensing. The board clamps requested intensity to the profile's haptic_max (1–255) and reports APPLIED_CLAMPED when it lowered a value (§4.3). DESIGN

5.4 Power

The C3 and Modulino use one USB-C feed; the ESP32-CAM gets a separate 5 V run from a pocket power bank. Common signal grounds are required. The listed Energizer bank supports simultaneous charging, but measured combined draw, brown-outs, thermal load and fit remain G0 checks. The LM3671 is sold out; TPS62827 is an unadopted 3.3 V rail candidate (3.4–5.5 V input), not the same pinout. An extra rail may be omitted only if G0 measurements support it. Integrated audio power requirements are unresolved.

GPIO budget: the C3 has one hardware I²C bus [59] for the Modulino Distance and the TCA9548A; the two DRV2605Ls sit on separate mux channels. No I²S pins are needed in v2. Fix the complete pin map and the status-LED GPIO before soldering; do not power the motors from an unmeasured C3 3V3 output. GATE G0

Rail assignment is a G0 decision: Modulino/mux logic from a verified C3 3V3 budget or a verified separate regulator; DRV2605L supply within vendor limits. Do not assume a regulator substitution or motor-drive redesign. The untested DT_HAPTIC_MOSFET flag has no selected shopping-list parts.

Chapter 7

Perception and hazard policy

7.1 Pipeline

  1. Ingest and freshness gate. Two independent producers: the ESP32-CAM's frames and the cue board's ToF samples each carry their board's clock, a per-board offset estimated by PING/PONG, and the phone's receive timestamp. Drop any input whose offset-corrected capture time or receive age exceeds the freshness bound. Every stage records a timestamp, for Gate G6.
  2. Forward range (class-agnostic). Only raw VL53L4CD status 0 yields VALID and a distance; every other raw status maps to UNKNOWN. This includes empty or out-of-range views: the sensor cannot distinguish them from a weak-signal target. The board and simulator never emit the protocol's NO_TARGET value. UNKNOWN means no supported distance, never far or clear; it enters announced DEGRADED and stops metric warning repeats. There are no zones, corridors, flanks or left/right range.
  3. Camera frames (not in the hazard loop). Frames are reassembled and kept only for the optional Describe path and the judge screen's frame age. There is no on-phone object detector, so no label, class or bearing is produced, and one forward zone could not be tied to a particular object in the frame anyway.
  4. Proximity band. FAR / MID / NEAR come from the single forward distance when it is valid; beyond 1200 mm nothing is reported, and that silence is not a clear path. Never speak metres without a valid sample.
  5. Policy. Pick at most one alert, with persistence and hysteresis (below).

7.2 Hazard policy (v0 rules)

inputs: range {mm, status}, head_yaw_rate

At 1.4 m/s, 250 ms of system latency plus about 1 s of human reaction consumes about 1.75 m of walking — more than the sensor's 1.2 m reach — so even FAR is a last-moment cue and this build supports a slow, staged near-field demo only. The 1.2 m figure is the vendor specification for a white indoor target [20]; it is not a validated useful warning distance, and gray or dark targets and bright sun shorten it (§5.1). INFERENCE One forward zone has no elevation resolution, so there is no low-return filtering, head-versus-chest zoning or mounting mask: it reports one distance for whatever is in its cone, and the cane's ground coverage is not modelled.

Chapter 9

Calibration for asymmetric hearing

Goal. Both sides of the cue should be equally perceptible to this wearer, with this frame position and this output device, without ever exceeding a hard ceiling. This is perceptual matching. It is not a hearing test or a diagnosis, and it does not restore hearing. DESIGN

  1. Fit and device. The app asks the wearer to seat the headset and requires the Bluetooth bone-conduction route to be the active cue output; if it is not, calibration refuses to start rather than playing tones from the phone speaker. It records the output device and the wearer's self-reported weaker side. It warns about ambient noise if the phone microphone reads high.
  2. Per-side detection threshold. An adaptive 2-down-1-up staircase, which converges near 70.7% correct [57], starts from a quiet level. The wearer answers "heard / not heard" by tapping. It runs in three bands that match the cue timbres (for example 500 Hz, 1 kHz and 2 kHz) and stops after 6–8 reversals. PROPOSED
  3. Presentation level. Per band and side, the level is threshold plus a sensation level chosen by the wearer between 10 and 20 dB [24], clamped to the ceiling.
  4. Loudness balance. Alternate the real "obstacle" cue left and right. The wearer nudges a balance control until both sides feel equally loud, which corrects for cue spectrum and for the bone crossing the skull. Repeat once to check consistency.
  5. Verify and choose a mode. Play five random-side calibration cues and have the wearer name the side. Mode values are phone-side audio semantics (wire mode: 0 STEREO, 1 SSD, 2 HAPTIC_ONLY); the cue board is haptic-only in every mode.
    • Stereo mode: at least 4 of 5 correct and no side at the ceiling.
    • SSD mode: one side cannot be detected at the ceiling, or side naming is at chance. Audio becomes identical on both sides (mono). Hazard cues carry no direction in v0 anyway, so SSD costs nothing in the demo; any future lateral information would have to ride haptics and spearcons.
    • Haptic-only mode: for wearers who prefer it or cannot use audio. No headset is required and warning sounds are off; temple haptics carry warnings. Phone vibration and the explicit system-off alarm remain failure signals. Hearing calibration still requires a headset.

If the headset disconnects or rejects a tone, calibration enters a failed state, discards its result and requires reconnection and a fresh session. An unplayed tone is never scored as unheard, and a failed or disconnected session cannot apply a profile.

9.1 Profile and safety rules

  • Ceiling first. No calibration step, slider or urgency step can exceed ceiling_db in the phone's audio path; the board separately caps haptic intensity at haptic_max, which the phone constrains to 1–255 — the haptic channel cannot be disabled because it is the last local channel left when the phone dies. If the weak side would need more, it is marked unusable. The app does not push more energy into it. The headset's own hardware volume remains outside the app's control, so the ceiling is relative to the volume set at calibration time. DESIGN
  • Re-run every session, because how the frame couples to the head changes each time it is worn. A 20-second quick check suffices if the previous profile is under 7 days old. PROPOSED
  • Dose. Track cumulative cue-on time. No final bone-conduction output has been selected or measured on an artificial mastoid; Determine cannot claim equivalence to WHO/ITU air-conduction limits [4][5].
  • Professional care. The app recommends an audiologist for anyone with known hearing loss and never displays results as audiometry.
  • OS features stay reachable. Android mono audio and balance settings remain available. Determine's profile works alongside them, not as a replacement.
